Here is a summary of the video **[This RSI Strategy Won 68% of 1,321 Trades](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tjL4wcI403E&utm_source=gemini)** by *Quantified Strategies*:

---

### Core Concept

The video presents a short-term **mean-reversion trading strategy** applied to individual stocks within the **NASDAQ 100** index (tested via Norgate data, accounting for delisted stocks) rather than trading the index ETF itself [[00:16](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D16)].

---

### Trading Rules

* **Trend Filter:** The stock's close must be **above its 200-day moving average** to ensure an overall bullish environment [[01:30](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D90)].
* **RSI Oversold Filter:** A **3-day RSI** must be **below 20** [[01:37](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D97)].
* **Intraday Weakness (IBS):** The **Internal Bar Strength (IBS)** indicator must be **less than 0.3**, signaling that the stock closed weak during the day [[01:52](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D112)].
* **Exit Rule (QS Exit):** Exit when the close is **higher than yesterday's high** [[01:59](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D119)].
* **Execution:** All buys and sells are executed at the **next open** with market orders [[02:06](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D126)].
* **Portfolio Limit:** A maximum of **5 open positions** at any given time [[02:19](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D139)].

---

### Backtest Performance

* **Total Trades:** 1,321 trades [[02:32](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D152)]
* **Win Rate:** 68% [[02:48](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D168)]
* **Average Gain Per Trade:** 1.1% [[02:42](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D162)]
* **Annualized Return:** ~11% [[02:56](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D176)]
* **Market Exposure:** Only invested around **16% of the time** (capital idle ~84% of the time) [[03:04](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D184)]
* **Maximum Drawdown:** 17% (notably low compared to NASDAQ's historical drawdowns) [[03:04](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D184)]

---

### Key Takeaways

1. **Capital Efficiency:** Because the strategy is only exposed to the market ~16% of the time, it frees up capital to be combined with other non-correlated systems (such as trend following or other mean-reversion setups) [[03:20](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D200)].
2. **Individual Stocks vs. Indices:** RSI-based mean reversion can produce substantial edges on individual equities, not just broad market ETFs like SPY [[03:47](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D227)].
3. **Caveats:** As with any backtest, slippage, liquidity, execution, and transaction costs should be considered in live execution [[04:13](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.youtube.com%2Fwatch%3Fv%3DtjL4wcI403E%26t%3D253)].

Here’s what the two layers of results mean together.

## 1. Stock book (baseline)
Buying the stock on the last-15m H2 signal:

- ~half the trades hit the ATR hard-stop (~−5%)
- ~half trail out, and a fat right tail of big winners carries the book
- Net: **n=2297, E +0.27%, PF 1.09**, median trade already **negative** (−3.4%)
- **2022 is bad** (−2.37 / 0.41)

So the edge is “lose often, win big sometimes,” not high win rate.

## 2. Black–Scholes short puts (paper)
Same signals, sell 45 DTE ATM put, TP at 50% of credit, stop when the stock hits ATR:

Under **constant IV** (no crash vol spike):

| | Stock | Short put (cash-secured %) |
|--|------:|---------------------------:|
| E | +0.27 | **+0.51** |
| PF | 1.09 | **1.51** |
| 2022 | red | **green** (+0.11) |

That looks better because:
- wins are capped (~3% of strike when you take 50% of premium)
- losses under constant IV were only ~−1.9% CS (much smaller than stock −5%)
- win rate rises (~49% vs ~31%)

Then we stressed stops by marking the put with **higher IV only on hard-stops**:

| Stop IV | E | PF | Meaning |
|---------|--:|---:|---------|
| ×1.0 | +0.51 | 1.51 | optimistic paper |
| ×1.25 | −0.27 | 0.85 | edge gone |
| ×1.5 | −1.05 | 0.59 | destroyed |

So the whole BS “win” lives in **how expensive the put is when you buy it back at the stock stop**. If real stops look like ×1.25 IV, don’t bother. If they look like ×1.0, puts might be interesting.

## 3. Free Polygon sample (reality check)
We pulled real **daily option closes** for 12 recent liquid hard-stops; **9** had usable prints.

Key number: **`live_exit / bs_exit`**

- median **0.85**
- all nine were **&lt; 1.1**
- **none** landed in the ×1.25 / ×1.5 kill zone

In plain English: on those stop days, the put’s traded close was **cheaper** to buy back than our constant-IV BS mark — the opposite of “IV blew out and crushed us.”

Live cash-secured P&amp;L on those stops was similar to BS (median ~−2.2% vs BS ~−1.8%), not catastrophically worse.

## 4. How to read that (and what it does *not* say)

**Supportive for puts (vs the IV-stress fear):**
- Real stop marks in this sample do **not** look like the stress that killed the BS book
- Constant-IV BS may have been **slightly harsh** on stops, not too kind

**Still not a green light:**
- Day **close ≠ ask** when you buy the put back (you usually pay more than the print)
- Entries also often printed **under** BS credit (you may collect less than paper assumed)
- Only **9** names, recent/liquid, free-tier gaps (3 had no usable bars)
- We still haven’t occupancy-walked / capital-sized a full live-options book
- Day bars can’t prove you actually fill at those prices in RTH when the stock tags the stop

## Bottom line
- **Stock:** fragile, fat-tailed, weak expectancy.
- **BS puts:** look better *only if* stop buybacks stay near constant IV.
- **Polygon sample:** those stop buybacks looked **~15% cheaper than BS**, not 25%+ more expensive — so the “IV spike kills it” fear didn’t show up in this free sample.

Next useful step if you want more conviction: larger sample and/or use **bid/ask** (or at least ask on exit) so fill realism isn’t still optimistic.

### What’s fairly solid
- **Signal clock** — same last-15m H2 arm as the stock book (known, causal).
- **Stop *trigger*** — underlying ATR k=2 is the same event as the stock hard-stop.
- **Polygon stop marks (n=9)** — live day-bar exits were **~0.85× BS**, not ×1.25+. So the “IV spike kills the book” stress didn’t show up on those liquid names. That’s the strongest real-data takeaway.

### What’s optimistic (makes puts look better than live)

| Assumption | Effect |
|------------|--------|
| Constant IV on the full BS book | Stops marked without crash vol / gap risk |
| Buyback at **day close** (Polygon) | Live you’d often pay the **ask** (worse) |
| Sell credit at day close / BS mid | Live you’d sell nearer the **bid** (worse) |
| Exact ATM + ~45 DTE synthetic expiry | Real chains may be weekly/monthly off a few days; thin names skip |
| No assignment / early exercise / dividend | Real short puts can get assigned into the stock |
| Cash-secured % vs stock % | Same-ish notional, but options liquidity and margin differ |
| Occupancy not re-walked | Concurrent short puts need more capital / risk limits |

So: BS **E +0.51 / PF 1.51** is **not** a live forecast. The ×1.25 IV stress (which kills it) is closer to a “bad fill / bad vol day” haircut than the free day-bar sample.

### What’s pessimistic / noise
- Polygon **entries** often printed **under** BS credit → paper may overstate premium collected.
- Thin tape (AVGO/ASML same-session open→close) can distort single-name P&amp;L.
- **n=9** liquid names ≠ the ~1100 hard-stops in the full book.

### Realism grades (rough)

| Layer | Realism | Trust for |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| Full BS ATM book | Low–medium | Ranking vs stock, IV sensitivity |
| IV×1.25 stress | Scenario, not truth | “What if stops are expensive?” |
| Polygon day-bar sample | Medium on **stop level**; low on **fill** | “Did stops blow out vs BS?” — no |
| Live short-put expectancy | Not established | Do not size capital from this |

### Bottom line
- **Realistic enough to say:** “stop IV alone doesn’t obviously destroy the idea on a few liquid names.”
- **Not realistic enough to say:** “short puts beat the stock book at +0.51% E.”
- To get closer to live: larger sample + **bid/ask** (or mid±half-spread) on entry *and* exit, listed expiry/strike matching, and a capital/occupancy walk.
